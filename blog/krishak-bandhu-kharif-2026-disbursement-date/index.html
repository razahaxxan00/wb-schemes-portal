Krishak Bandhu Kharif 2026 Disbursement Date — What Farmers Need to Know

West Bengal farmers waiting on their Kharif 2026 Krishak Bandhu installment can expect disbursement in the usual June–July window, following the same seasonal pattern the scheme has followed since 2019. This update covers what's confirmed, how the treasury-based payment system works, and what to do if your installment hasn't arrived yet.

Read more →

Krishak Bandhu's Two-Installment Cycle

Krishak Bandhu pays eligible farmers in two instalments every year, tied to India's two main cropping seasons:

  • Kharif installment — typically released in the June–July window, supporting farmers ahead of the monsoon sowing season
  • Rabi installment — typically released in the December–January window, though timing has occasionally shifted into early spring depending on administrative processing

For the current cycle, the Rabi 2026 installment began reaching beneficiary bank accounts from 8 March 2026, timed to coincide with Krishak Divas (Farmers' Day). Following that same seasonal rhythm, the Kharif 2026 installment is expected between June and July 2026, though exact disbursement dates for individual farmers can vary by a few weeks depending on district-level treasury processing.

How Much to Expect

The disbursement amount depends on landholding size:

  • Farmers owning one acre or more of cultivable land receive ₹10,000 per year, split into two equal instalments of ₹5,000 each
  • Farmers with less than one acre receive support on a pro-rata basis, with a guaranteed minimum of ₹4,000 per year, also split across both instalments

So for the Kharif 2026 cycle specifically, eligible landholding farmers should expect to see ₹5,000 credited (or a smaller pro-rata amount for sub-one-acre holdings).

Why Krishak Bandhu Payments Work Differently From Central Schemes

If you've also used central government schemes like PM-KISAN, it's worth understanding one key difference: PM-KISAN uses a direct Aadhaar-linked Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) system, crediting funds from the central treasury directly. Krishak Bandhu, by contrast, is disbursed through West Bengal's own state treasury system, releasing funds through district-specific treasuries rather than a single centralised transfer. This is one reason disbursement can appear to roll out gradually, district by district, rather than landing in every eligible farmer's account on exactly the same date — even when the state government has already made the official announcement that funds have been released.

How to Check Your Kharif 2026 Status

  1. Visit the official Krishak Bandhu portal at krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in
  2. Look for the "Check Payment Status" or "Know Your Status" section
  3. Enter your Voter ID (EPIC number) or Aadhaar number as registered with the scheme
  4. Submit to view your current status — pending, processed, or credited — along with the relevant installment details

If you don't have your Krishak Bandhu ID readily available, the same "Know Your Status" lookup using your Voter ID or Aadhaar number will typically display your registered ID alongside your payment details.

What to Do If Your Payment Hasn't Arrived

Before assuming there's a problem specific to you, keep in mind:

  • Treasury-based rollout takes time. Because disbursement happens district by district, farmers in different parts of the state may see their credit at different times within the same announced window, even after an official release date has been confirmed.
  • Bank account and Aadhaar mismatch is the most common individual-level issue. If your name, account number, or Aadhaar details don't precisely match your registered application, your specific payment can be held back even while your neighbour's goes through normally.
  • Incorrect Voter ID/Aadhaar entry at registration can also cause a payment to be flagged for correction rather than processed automatically.

If your status shows "pending" or "rejected" well after the announced disbursement window has opened in your district, the recommended next step is to request a correction or re-verification through the official portal, or visit your local agriculture office with your Krishak Bandhu ID, Aadhaar card, and Voter ID on hand.

A Word of Caution on Unofficial Updates

Around each disbursement cycle, it's common to see farmers sharing payment screenshots on WhatsApp and social media once credits begin appearing in some areas. While these local reports can be a useful early signal that a rollout has started, they aren't a substitute for checking your own status directly on the official portal or confirming with your bank. Treat unverified claims about disbursement dates, scheme changes, or "final chance" application deadlines circulating on social media or messaging platforms with caution, and rely on the official Krishak Bandhu portal or your bank for confirmation of your own payment status.

Has the Scheme Amount Changed for 2026?

As of this update, there is no official notification of any increase or structural change to the Krishak Bandhu benefit amount for the Kharif 2026 cycle. The scheme continues to operate on the same ₹10,000/₹4,000 annual structure (split across Kharif and Rabi instalments) that has applied since its 2019 relaunch as "Krishak Bandhu (Natun)." Any future revision to benefit amounts or eligibility would be announced through official government channels — this page will be updated if and when that happens.

Documents and Details Worth Keeping Ready

Whether you're checking your status online or need to visit your local agriculture office to resolve a delay, having these details ready in advance saves a repeat trip:

  • Your registered Voter ID (EPIC) number or Aadhaar number
  • Your Krishak Bandhu Smart Card or ID, if one has been issued to you
  • Your bank passbook or account statement, to confirm the account number matches what's registered with the scheme
  • Land ownership or Khatian documents (for landowners), or supporting proof of cultivation (for sharecroppers and agricultural labourers registered under the scheme)

Farmers who are sharecroppers or agricultural labourers rather than landowners remain eligible for Krishak Bandhu support, but should specifically confirm their registration reflects that status correctly, since land-based and non-land-based registrations are sometimes processed slightly differently at the district level.

What Happens After the Kharif Window Closes

Once the Kharif 2026 disbursement window has run its course across all districts, attention typically shifts to the Rabi 2026-27 cycle, expected in the following December–January window. Farmers who successfully received their Kharif instalment don't need to reapply for Rabi — Krishak Bandhu registration remains active year to year as long as your land records and bank details stay current, with the main exception being if your Voter ID, Aadhaar, or bank details have changed since your last successful payment, in which case an update through the official portal or your local block office is advisable before the next cycle opens.
