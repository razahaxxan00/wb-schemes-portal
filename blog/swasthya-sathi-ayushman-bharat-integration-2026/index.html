Swasthya Sathi & Ayushman Bharat Integration 2026 — What It Means for Your Card

West Bengal officially joined the central Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) scheme on 1 July 2026, becoming the 36th and final state/union territory in India to do so. This marks the end of a seven-year period in which the state ran its own standalone Swasthya Sathi programme instead of joining the central scheme. This update explains exactly what's changing, what stays the same, and what you need to check for your own coverage.

Read more →

What Actually Happened

On 8 June 2026, the West Bengal government signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the National Health Authority in New Delhi to bring Ayushman Bharat into the state. Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari made the announcement following discussions with the Union Health Minister, reversing the previous state government's seven-year decision to stay outside the central scheme. The rollout went live on 1 July 2026, backed by a central healthcare package of approximately ₹3,505 crore for West Bengal.

Around 6 crore people currently holding Swasthya Sathi cards are being migrated into the Ayushman Bharat ecosystem as part of this transition.

The Most Important Thing to Understand: Eligibility Rules Are Different

This is the single most critical point for existing Swasthya Sathi cardholders to understand, and it's widely misunderstood: Swasthya Sathi and Ayushman Bharat don't use the same eligibility criteria.

  • Swasthya Sathi was designed to be near-universal, covering the overwhelming majority of West Bengal households regardless of income level, with no cap on family size.
  • Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY) uses the SECC 2011 (Socio-Economic Caste Census) database, a narrower, poverty-focused eligibility list, the same database used nationwide for PM-JAY across every other state.

This means not every Swasthya Sathi beneficiary automatically qualifies under Ayushman Bharat's central criteria. You should check your specific PM-JAY eligibility rather than assuming your Swasthya Sathi coverage transfers over unchanged.

What Happens If You Don't Qualify Under Central Criteria

For Swasthya Sathi beneficiaries who don't meet the central government's SECC 2011 criteria, West Bengal has introduced a state-funded bridge scheme called the Mukhyamantri Swasthya Bima Yojana (MMSBY). Under this arrangement, the state government bears the full premium cost to onboard these beneficiaries onto the Ayushman Bharat platform, aiming to provide comparable benefits even though they fall outside the central scheme's standard eligibility rules.

As of this update, detailed operational guidelines and specific enrolment dates for MMSBY had not yet been announced. If you're unsure which category you fall into, the safest approach is to check your status through official channels rather than assuming either outcome.

What's Genuinely Better Under Ayushman Bharat

The transition isn't purely administrative, it brings one major, tangible upgrade for West Bengal residents:

Nationwide portability. Swasthya Sathi coverage was largely limited to hospitals empanelled within West Bengal. Ayushman Bharat cardholders can access cashless treatment at any of the 36,000+ empanelled public and private hospitals across India, including major institutions like AIIMS and CMC Vellore. This is a significant benefit for the roughly 1 crore Bengali migrant workers currently living and working in other states, who previously had no cashless coverage outside West Bengal under Swasthya Sathi alone.

Coverage itself remains at ₹5 lakh per family per year for cashless treatment, consistent with what Swasthya Sathi previously offered.

Is Your Existing Swasthya Sathi Card Still Valid?

Yes, during the transition period, existing Swasthya Sathi smart cards remain valid and usable. When you need hospitalisation, you can present either your Swasthya Sathi smart card or your Ayushman Bharat card at the hospital's help desk, depending on which you've received so far. You do not need to discard or stop using your Swasthya Sathi card simply because the transition has been announced.

What You Should Do Now

  • Check your PM-JAY eligibility separately rather than assuming automatic coverage, this is the most commonly misunderstood part of the transition.
  • Watch for eKYC and beneficiary verification requests. As Ayushman Bharat cards are issued, beneficiaries are being asked to complete eKYC (electronic Know Your Customer) verification; follow official instructions when you receive one rather than ignoring it.
  • Keep using your existing Swasthya Sathi card for now if you haven't yet received an Ayushman Bharat card, it remains valid during the rollout.
  • If you fall outside SECC 2011 criteria, keep an eye on official MMSBY announcements for how the state-funded bridge coverage will be enrolled, since detailed guidelines were still pending as of this update.
  • Continue checking hospital empanelment lists if you're planning treatment, since private hospital onboarding into the new network was still actively accelerating around the July 2026 launch.

Why This Matters Beyond the Political Change

Whatever the political context behind the decision, the practical outcome for residents is a shift from a purely state-administered scheme to one integrated with a national framework, with the trade-off being a stricter, income-based eligibility test for full central-scheme benefits, offset by significantly wider hospital access across India. For families with members working outside the state, or anyone who might need treatment while travelling, the portability upgrade is likely to be the most immediately useful change.

How This Compares to Other States That Joined PM-JAY Earlier

West Bengal's transition follows a pattern seen in other large states that joined AB PM-JAY in earlier years: an initial period of parallel operation, where the older state scheme and the new central scheme run side by side, followed by a gradual narrowing as beneficiary databases are reconciled and state-specific bridge schemes (like MMSBY here) are formalised to cover the gap population. States that completed this transition previously generally saw the bridge-scheme enrolment process take several months to fully stabilise, with early confusion over which card to present at hospitals gradually resolving as eKYC verification and card issuance caught up with demand. West Bengal, being the last state to join, has the advantage of a more mature national rollout process and established hospital empanelment infrastructure already in place from other states' experience, which may help shorten that adjustment period compared to earlier state transitions.
