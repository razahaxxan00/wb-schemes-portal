Banglar Bari Prakalpa — West Bengal's Rural Housing Scheme

Fact-Checked 2026 Fact-checked against official state notifications (wb.gov.in) • Editorial Review: August 2026

Banglar Bari ("Bengal's House") is a state-funded rural housing scheme run by the Government of West Bengal, providing financial assistance to families living in kutcha (temporary) houses or without any home of their own, so they can build a permanent pucca house.

Read more →

What is Banglar Bari Scheme?

The West Bengal government launched Banglar Bari as its own state-funded housing programme for the rural poor, delivering grants directly to beneficiaries' bank accounts. In urban areas, the scheme is implemented by the Housing Department; in rural areas, by the Panchayat and Rural Development Department. Beneficiaries are identified through a government survey, after which provisional Eligible, Ineligible, and Inactive lists are published block and gram-panchayat wise for verification.

Banglar Bari Eligibility Criteria

  • Must be a permanent resident of West Bengal
  • Must belong to the Economically Weaker Section (EWS) category
  • Must be currently living in a kutcha (temporary/mud) house, a damaged house, or be without a home of their own
  • Must not already own a pucca house in the name of any family member
  • Selected through a government household survey, families aren't required to apply individually in most cases, but can raise claims/objections if missed from the list

Banglar Bari Scheme Benefits

  • Total assistance of ₹1,20,000 per beneficiary to construct a house, paid in instalments (commonly a first instalment of around ₹60,000, followed by further releases)
  • Higher assistance, around ₹1,30,000, for beneficiaries in remote areas like Jangalmahal and the Darjeeling Hills
  • Funds transferred directly to the beneficiary's bank account via Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)
  • Aims to replace mud/kutcha houses with durable, permanent structures across the state

How Beneficiary Identification Actually Works

Unlike schemes where individuals submit an application and wait for approval, Banglar Bari primarily identifies beneficiaries through a structured government survey conducted at the gram panchayat and block level. Survey teams assess housing conditions, family income indicators, and existing government scheme enrolment to compile an initial list of potentially eligible households. This list is then published as a Provisional List, giving affected households the opportunity to review their status before it's finalised.

The Claims and Objections Process

After the provisional list is published, there's typically a defined window during which:

  • Households who believe they were wrongly excluded can raise a claim to be included
  • Households or other community members can raise an objection to a listed household's inclusion, if they have specific grounds to believe that household doesn't meet the criteria (for example, evidence of an existing pucca house not disclosed during the survey)

This claims and objections process is a standard feature of large-scale beneficiary identification schemes in West Bengal, designed to catch both wrongful exclusions and wrongful inclusions before the final list and fund disbursement.

Instalment Structure in Detail

Assistance under Banglar Bari isn't paid as a single lump sum, it's released in instalments tied to construction progress:

  • First instalment (commonly around ₹60,000) is released once a beneficiary is confirmed on the final eligible list and basic groundwork requirements are met
  • Subsequent instalments follow as construction progresses, verified through physical inspection or photographic evidence submitted by the beneficiary or verified by local officials
  • The final instalment is typically released once construction reaches a defined completion stage

This phased approach is intended to ensure funds are actually being used for genuine construction progress, rather than disbursed as a single payment with no follow-up verification.

Common Reasons for Delayed Instalments

If a beneficiary has received their first instalment but subsequent releases are delayed, common causes include:

  • Construction progress hasn't yet been verified by the local officer responsible for inspection
  • Required photographic or documentary evidence of progress hasn't been submitted
  • A backlog in local administrative processing during periods of high disbursement volume across many beneficiaries simultaneously

What Happens If Construction Isn't Completed

Beneficiaries are generally expected to complete construction within a reasonable timeframe following fund release. Significant, unexplained delays in construction progress can affect the release of subsequent instalments, since the phased disbursement model is specifically designed around demonstrated progress. If personal circumstances (illness, financial hardship beyond the scheme's assistance, etc.) are genuinely preventing progress, raising this with your Gram Panchayat or BDO office directly is advisable rather than letting the application go inactive without explanation.
