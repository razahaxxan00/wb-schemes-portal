Beneficiary List Guide

Krishak Bandhu Beneficiary List — How to Check Online (2026)

Fact-Checked 2026 Fact-checked against official state notifications (krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in) • Editorial Review: August 2026

What Is the Krishak Bandhu Beneficiary List

The Krishak Bandhu beneficiary list is the official, publicly viewable record of farmers who have been verified and approved under the Krishak Bandhu scheme by the Department of Agriculture, Government of West Bengal. Appearing on this krishak bandhu beneficiary list confirms that your application has cleared local verification by the Block Development Officer (BDO) and Assistant Director of Agriculture (ADA), and that you're recognised as an eligible beneficiary for that Gram Panchayat.

Unlike a private login, this list is organised by geography — district, block, and Gram Panchayat — rather than by individual account, so checking it is slightly different from checking your payment status.

Many farmers use this list simply to confirm they're "on record" after registering for Krishak Bandhu, separately from checking whether a specific payment has actually landed in their Aadhaar-linked bank account.

Under the state's public transparency norms, Gram Panchayat offices also display hardcopy physical lists on local bulletin boards prior to seasonal Kharif and Rabi disbursement cycles.

The beneficiary database is maintained electronically within the Integrated Financial Management System (IFMS) to prevent duplicate applications across adjacent Gram Panchayats. Every entry on the beneficiary schedule undergoes systematic three-tier verification before final inclusion: initial field authentication by Krishi Technology Sahayaks (KTS), document scrutiny by the Assistant Director of Agriculture (ADA), and treasury queue authorization by the Block Development Officer (BDO). This ensures that genuine landowning farmers, sharecroppers (Bhagchasi), and Patta holders receive their direct cash assistance transparently.

How to Check the List Online, Step by Step

  1. Visit the official portal: krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in.
  2. Locate the Beneficiary List or Beneficiary Status section, usually linked from the homepage or a dedicated menu.
  3. You'll typically be asked to narrow your search using location filters — district, block, and Gram Panchayat.
  4. Once you've selected the correct Gram Panchayat, the portal displays a list of registered, approved farmers for that area.
  5. Scroll or use your browser's find function (Ctrl+F on desktop) to search for your name more quickly if the list is long.
  6. Where available, some portal versions also let you search directly by Voter ID number instead of manually scrolling — this is faster if your Gram Panchayat's list is large.

Online lists display the registered farmer's name, father's/husband's name, village, land holding category, and masked bank account reference for privacy compliance.

Farmers without personal computer access can visit their nearest Bangla Sahayata Kendra (BSK) for free digital lookup assistance.

Printed master beneficiary registers are also verified annually by the District Magistrate's office before approving the district-wide annual financial allocation under the Krishak Bandhu (Natun) budget.

You can print or download the Gram Panchayat beneficiary schedule directly from the portal for your personal documentation, or request a certified copy from your local Panchayat Pradhan during official office hours.

Searching by District, Block & Gram Panchayat

The beneficiary list is structured hierarchically to match West Bengal's local administrative divisions:

  • District — select your district from the top-level dropdown menu.
  • Block — each district contains several blocks; choose the one your village or area falls under.
  • Gram Panchayat (GP) — the smallest unit shown; this is where the actual beneficiary names are listed.

If you're not sure exactly which block or Gram Panchayat your village belongs to, your local Krishi Bhavan or Panchayat office can confirm this — it's worth knowing for future reference, since several other West Bengal welfare schemes use the same administrative hierarchy for their own beneficiary lists.

Knowing your exact Mouza name and JL number (found on your land Record of Rights / Parcha) also helps prevent confusion between villages with similar names in the same Gram Panchayat.

In agricultural blocks with large populations of registered Bhagchasi (sharecroppers), names are categorized separately under Raiyat (landowners) and Barga (sharecroppers) tabs for ease of administrative auditing.

For municipal or semi-urban village areas bordering municipal corporations, select the adjacent block authority where your agricultural land plot is officially registered in land records, as urban local bodies do not maintain separate agricultural beneficiary schedules.

My Name Isn't on the List — What to Do

If you've submitted your application when applying online or at a Duare Sarkar camp but can't find your name:

  1. Give it time first. Verification against official land Records of Rights (RoR Parcha) commonly takes a few weeks, and lists are usually updated in batches rather than continuously — a recent application may simply not have appeared yet.
  2. Double-check your location selection. It's easy to select a neighbouring Gram Panchayat by mistake, especially in areas where GP boundaries don't line up neatly with village names.
  3. Try the individual status-check route instead. The status-check page looks up your specific Voter ID rather than requiring you to browse a list, which can be more reliable if the list itself is long or slow to load.
  4. Visit your Krishi Bhavan in person if it's been an unusually long wait. Bring your original registration acknowledgment receipt. Staff can tell you directly whether your application is still pending, was rejected (and why), or whether there's simply a listing batch delay.

If an application was flagged due to land record mismatch or pending Barga certificate re-verification, block officials can unlock your electronic record to allow document resubmission.

Farmers whose names were omitted due to recent land mutation changes should submit a copy of their updated Khatian RoR to the Krishi Technology Sahayak (KTS) to refresh their master database entry.

If you submitted your physical form at a Duare Sarkar outreach camp, present your stamped acknowledgment slip to the block computer operator to verify whether your offline file has been indexed into the central portal database.

Beneficiary List vs Status Check — Which Should You Use

These two Krishak Bandhu tools answer slightly different questions, and it helps to know which one you actually need:

  • Use the Beneficiary List (this page) when you want to confirm you're officially registered and approved for your Gram Panchayat.
  • Use Status Check when you want to know about your individual payment history — specific instalment dates and amounts credited to your account under the ₹10,000/year (1+ acre) or ₹4,000/year (<1 acre) benefit rules.

If your real question is "has my money arrived," go straight to Status Check rather than searching the beneficiary list, which won't show payment transaction details.

Both systems update automatically prior to seasonal Kharif and Rabi funds release cycles, ensuring public visibility across all 23 districts of West Bengal.

Combining both tools gives you a full picture of your enrolment: the beneficiary list verifies your official standing in the state database, while the status check tracks every Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) credit into your bank account. Farmers are advised to check the beneficiary list at the start of each agricultural season (Kharif in May–June and Rabi in November–December) to ensure their record remains active following periodic departmental audits and land mutation updates across West Bengal.
