How to Apply for Krishak Bandhu — Online & Offline (2026)

Fact-Checked 2026 Fact-checked against official state notifications (krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in) • Editorial Review: August 2026

Before You Apply: Eligibility & Documents Checklist

Before starting your Krishak Bandhu application, confirm you meet the basic eligibility described on the main scheme page: you should be a permanent resident of West Bengal, either a landowning farmer or a registered Bhagchasi (sharecropper), actively engaged in cultivation. First, check your eligibility to ensure all criteria are satisfied.

Gather these documents in advance so the process goes smoothly in one sitting:

  • Land record — Record of Rights (RoR Parcha), RoR with Barga registration, a Patta record, or a recent Forest Patta, matching whichever category applies to you.
  • Aadhaar card, with your name spelled exactly as on your other documents.
  • Voter ID card.
  • Bank passbook — the first page showing your name, account number, and IFSC code, for an account linked to your Aadhaar.
  • Recent passport-size photograph.
  • Active mobile number you have access to right now, for OTP verification.

Having mismatched spellings of your name across these documents is one of the more common, avoidable causes of delay — it's worth checking this before you begin your krishak bandhu apply online submission.

Sharecroppers applying under Operation Barga must verify that their Barga registration certificate number is active in the Land & Land Reforms Department database before filling in the application.

Under the revised Krishak Bandhu guidelines, eligible farmers receive ₹10,000 per year (for 1 acre or more) or a minimum of ₹4,000 per year (for less than 1 acre), plus ₹2 Lakh death benefit coverage. All financial disbursements require an active single-operated bank account linked to your Aadhaar NPCI mapper.

How to Apply for Krishak Bandhu Online (Step-by-Step)

Follow this exact step-by-step procedure to register on the web portal:

  1. Go to krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in in your web browser.
  2. Select New Registration from the farmer services section.
  3. Enter your active 10-digit mobile number to receive a one-time password (OTP).
  4. Enter the OTP promptly — it typically expires within a few minutes — to unlock the application form interface.
  5. Fill in your personal details (full name, date of birth, father's/husband's name, gender, category, and address) exactly as they appear on your Aadhaar and Voter ID.
  6. Enter your land record details — Mouza name, JL number, plot number, Khatian/RoR number, and total area under cultivation — exactly as recorded officially in your Parcha. This is the single most common source of application mismatches, so double-check every digit.
  7. Enter your bank account details (bank name, branch name, account number, and IFSC code), matching your passbook precisely.
  8. Upload scanned copies or clear photographs of each required document — make sure text is legible and the full document is visible in frame, not cropped.
  9. Use the review screen to check every field before final submission — corrections after submission are often harder than getting it right the first time.
  10. Submit the form. Note down your registration/application number — you'll need this to track your application status later, so save it somewhere safe.

After online submission, a digital receipt is generated with a unique reference tracking ID. Save a PDF copy of this receipt for your personal files.

The electronic portal transmits your submitted records directly to the Block Agriculture Office system. An automated SMS notification confirms that your file has entered the verification queue.

How to Download and Fill the Application Form (PDF)

If you'd rather complete a paper form using a krishak bandhu form pdf:

  1. Visit krishakbandhu.wb.gov.in and look for a Download Form link — or collect a printed copy directly, free of cost, at your nearest Krishi Bhavan.
  2. Print the form on plain A4 paper if downloading digitally.
  3. Fill it in clearly, in block capital letters where possible, to avoid processing delays caused by illegible handwriting.
  4. Attach self-attested photocopies (sign across a corner of each copy) of all the documents listed in Section 1.
  5. Do a final check that every field is complete — an incomplete form is one of the most common reasons for delay at the counter.

Form submissions require two recent passport photos signed on the front or back. Ensure your mobile number is clearly written on top of the form for SMS updates.

If a family member is acting as a legal representative due to the primary farmer's physical illness or age, attach a certified authorization slip signed by the local Panchayat Pradhan.

Applying Offline at a Krishi Bhavan or Duare Sarkar Camp

  1. Locate your nearest Block Agriculture Office (Krishi Bhavan), or find out if an active Duare Sarkar camp is currently running in your area — these camps periodically visit villages specifically to process welfare-scheme applications.
  2. Bring your completed form and all original documents (plus the photocopies) — staff will typically verify originals against your photocopies on the spot.
  3. Submit the form and ask for a written acknowledgment or receipt with a reference number.
  4. Keep this receipt safe — it's your proof of submission and the reference you'll use when checking the beneficiary list later.

Duare Sarkar camps feature dedicated Krishi Department counters equipped with digital data-entry operators who immediately upload your physical application into the central IFMS verification portal.

Field inspection officers (Krishi Technology Sahayaks) conduct on-site verification of cultivated plots for sharecroppers and Patta holders to authenticate physical possession.

Common Mistakes That Cause Rejection

  • Name spelling mismatches between Aadhaar, Voter ID, land records, and bank passbook.
  • Incorrect or outdated land record numbers — always use your most recent RoR, not an old family record that hasn't been updated.
  • Bank account not linked to Aadhaar — even a technically correct account number will fail Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) if the Aadhaar-seeding step wasn't completed with your bank.
  • Blurry or partially cropped document photos, especially for the land record and Aadhaar, which need every field clearly readable.
  • Incomplete Bhagchasi (sharecropper) registration — if you're applying as a registered sharecropper rather than a landowner, make sure your Barga registration itself is up to date before you apply for Krishak Bandhu, not after.

Double-checking your documents against these five points before submitting saves weeks of back-and-forth correction visits at your local block office.

What Happens After You Submit

Your application is forwarded to the local agriculture office, where staff verify your details against official land records. This typically takes a few weeks, though timelines can vary by district and by how close you are to an upcoming payment cycle. Once verified and approved, you're added to the beneficiary database and your instalments begin from the next scheduled Kharif or Rabi payment round.

Under the scheme's revised guidelines, eligible farmers receive ₹10,000 per year (for 1 acre or more) or a minimum of ₹4,000 per year (for less than 1 acre), plus ₹2 Lakh death benefit coverage. There is no application fee at any stage of this process — registration is completely free through official channels, whether online, by form, or at a Duare Sarkar camp.

The Assistant Director of Agriculture (ADA) conducts final sign-off, after which your bank account is queued for Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) credit during the seasonal batch release.
