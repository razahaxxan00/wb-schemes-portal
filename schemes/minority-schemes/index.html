Minority Welfare Schemes in West Bengal (2026)

Fact-Checked 2026 Fact-checked against official WBMDFC notifications (wbmdfc.org) • Editorial Review: August 2026

Complete guide to West Bengal government programmes for notified minority communities — covering Aikyashree scholarships, WBMDFC self-employment and education loans, madrasah grants, and infrastructure support.

Government Support for West Bengal's Minority Communities

West Bengal runs one of the more extensive sets of state-level minority welfare programmes in India, covering education, self-employment, housing infrastructure, and community welfare for its notified minority communities — Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, and Parsi residents. Most of these programmes are administered by the West Bengal Minorities' Development and Finance Corporation (WBMDFC), established in 1996 under the West Bengal Minorities Development and Finance Corporation Act, working alongside the state's Minority Affairs and Madrasah Education Department.

This page is a guide to what's currently available under this category — starting with the largest and best-documented programme, the Aikyashree Scholarship, and then covering WBMDFC's other support schemes, which don't yet have their own dedicated deep-dive pages on this site but are well worth knowing about.

Aikyashree Scholarship — the Flagship Programme

The Aikyashree Scholarship is West Bengal's umbrella scholarship system for minority-community students, covering education from Class 1 all the way through PhD level. It isn't one single scholarship but five separate components bundled under one portal — Pre-Matric, Post-Matric, Merit-cum-Means (MCM), the Talent Support Programme (TSP), and the Swami Vivekananda Merit-cum-Means Scholarship (SVMCM) — each with its own eligibility rules and benefit amounts.

Eligibility generally requires West Bengal domicile, belonging to a notified minority community, and meeting a minimum marks requirement in your most recent qualifying exam (commonly around 50% for Pre-Matric/Post-Matric, with higher thresholds for MCM and professional courses). Family income ceilings also apply, varying by component.

WBMDFC Self-Employment Term Loans

For minority-community residents looking to start or expand a small business, WBMDFC offers term loans at concessional interest rates, generally in the range of 3–5%, well below typical market rates for unsecured small-business lending. Based on multiple district government sources:

  • Age Eligibility: 18 to 50 years.
  • Loan Margin Money: Broadly up to around ₹98,000 for rural applicants and up to around ₹1,20,000 for urban applicants (exact current limits should be confirmed with WBMDFC directly, since these figures can be revised).
  • Guarantor Requirement: A guarantor is typically required for larger loan amounts (for instance, loans between roughly ₹1,00,000 and ₹5,00,000).
  • Where to Apply: Rural applicants generally apply through their local BDO (Block Development Officer) and Gram Panchayat office; urban applicants apply through the SDO (Sub-Divisional Officer) office.
  • Application Window: Applications are typically accepted throughout the year for rural applications, subject to local processing schedules.

This scheme is aimed specifically at economic self-reliance — rather than a grant, it's a loan intended to help minority-community individuals establish small trades, shops, or service businesses with more accessible financing terms than they might get elsewhere.

WBMDFC Education Loans

Separate from the Aikyashree scholarship system, WBMDFC also offers dedicated education loans for minority-community students pursuing higher and professional studies, including Medical, Engineering, Management, and Nursing courses:

  • Loan Amount: Up to roughly ₹20 lakh for study within India, and up to roughly ₹30 lakh for study abroad.
  • Interest Rate: Concessional, generally cited in the range of 3–5%.
  • Application Window: Typically opens annually, broadly around June to July, through the WBMDFC website (wbmdfc.org).

This is a genuine loan (to be repaid), distinct from the Aikyashree scholarship components, which are non-repayable financial assistance — students sometimes confuse the two, so it's worth being clear you're applying for the correct one for your situation.

Minority Hostel & Infrastructure Support

Beyond direct financial assistance to individuals, the state also funds infrastructure development for minority-community education, through the Directorate of Madrasah Education and related bodies:

  • Construction of hostels for minority boys and girls, intended to support students from areas without nearby access to secondary or higher-secondary education.
  • Building grants for additional classrooms, laboratories, libraries, and basic facilities like drinking water and toilets, particularly for girls, at Madrasahs and minority-run institutions.
  • Support for NGOs running minority-focused educational projects, with financial assistance reportedly available up to roughly ₹50 lakh for infrastructure development, subject to the specific scheme's current terms.

Applications for NGO-level infrastructure support are typically submitted online through the West Bengal Minority Affairs Department's dedicated NGO scheme portal.

Girls' Incentive Grant for Madrasah Students

To encourage continued schooling for girls specifically, the state provides an annual incentive grant to girl students of Madrasahs studying in Class IX through XII — historically cited at around ₹1,200 per year, alongside smaller grants (such as a school dress allowance, historically around ₹600) for poorer students in earlier classes (Class V to VIII). These grants are administered locally through the Madrasah education system and are separate from the Aikyashree scholarship components.

Pradhan Mantri Jan Vikas Karyakram (PMJVK)

PMJVK (formerly known as the Multi-Sectoral Development Programme, or MsDP) is a joint Centre-State infrastructure initiative aimed at improving living conditions in identified minority-concentration areas across West Bengal. Rather than providing direct benefits to individuals, PMJVK funds broader community infrastructure — better school facilities, healthcare access, sanitation, roads, drinking water, and pucca (permanent) housing — as well as income-generating opportunity programmes, in areas where minority communities are concentrated and existing infrastructure gaps are greatest.

How These Schemes Fit Together

If you're trying to work out which of these programmes is relevant to you, it generally comes down to what you need:

  • Currently studying, need scholarship support: Apply for the Aikyashree Scholarship.
  • Want to start or grow a small business: Apply for a WBMDFC Self-Employment Term Loan.
  • Pursuing higher/professional education, need a loan (not a grant): Apply for a WBMDFC Education Loan.
  • Looking for hostel accommodation or your institution needs infrastructure funding: Explore Minority Hostel & Infrastructure Support, or the NGO-level PMJVK scheme if you represent an organisation rather than an individual.
  • A girl studying in a Madrasah, Class IX–XII: Access the Girls' Incentive Grant, alongside whichever Aikyashree component also applies to your class level.

WBMDFC Micro-Finance Direct Lending to Minority Women SHGs

In addition to individual term loans, WBMDFC provides micro-credit to women belonging to notified minority communities:

  • Direct Group Credit (up to ₹30,000 per member): Minority Self-Help Groups (SHGs) registered under Anandadhara / NRLM receive direct micro-finance loans at a 3% annual interest rate to support tailoring, handicraft, and food processing micro-units.
  • Simplified Group Guarantee: Requires no individual collateral; loans are disbursed to the SHG bank account based on group solidarity and repayment history.

Haj House Infrastructure and Cultural Welfare Initiatives

The Minority Affairs and Madrasah Education Department oversees dedicated cultural and pilgrimage infrastructure across West Bengal:

  • State Haj Committee & Transit Infrastructure: Operates modern Haj Houses at Rajarhat, Kolkata, providing free lodging, visa processing desks, medical check-ups, and transit support for pilgrims.
  • Aliah University Academic Empowerment: Supports higher research, technical education, and language studies through autonomous state minority universities.
